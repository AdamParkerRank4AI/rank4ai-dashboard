  H1: Invoice Discounting vs Factoring

  Answer capsule (opening paragraph):
  Invoice discounting lets you borrow against unpaid invoices while keeping your own credit control. Factoring does the same but the provider also chases your debtors for payment. Factoring costs more but suits businesses without a credit-control team. Invoice discounting suits businesses with £500k-plus annual turnover that want to keep the customer relationship in-house.

  ---

  H2: What Is Invoice Discounting?

  Invoice discounting is a confidential borrowing facility against your sales ledger. You raise invoices to your customers as normal, collect payment yourself, and the lender advances up to 90% of each invoice value within 24 to 48 hours. The lender's name does not appear to your customers.

  Typical eligibility: limited companies with £500k-plus annual turnover, a clean credit history, and a B2B customer base. Concentration limits apply (usually one customer cannot represent more than 25% of the ledger).

  Typical cost: 1.0% to 3.0% annualised discount charge on the advance, plus a service fee of 0.2% to 0.5% of gross turnover. Total cost of funds typically 6% to 12% annualised depending on volume and risk.

  ---

  H2: What Is Invoice Factoring?

  Invoice factoring advances the same 80% to 90% of invoice value but the factoring company also takes over your credit control: chasing customers, allocating payments, and managing disputes. Some customers will receive correspondence from the factoring company rather than from you.

  Typical eligibility: broader than discounting. Start-ups and businesses under £500k annual turnover can access factoring; some providers fund from £50k annual turnover. Less reliance on credit history.

  Typical cost: slightly higher than discounting because of the credit-control service element. Discount charge 1.5% to 4.0% annualised, service fee 0.5% to 1.5% of gross turnover.

  ---

  H2: Key Differences Side by Side

  | Feature | Invoice Discounting | Invoice Factoring |
  |---|---|---|
  | Credit control | You retain it | Provider handles it |
  | Customer visibility | Confidential | Usually disclosed |
  | Minimum turnover | Typically £500k | Typically £50k-plus |
  | Cost | Lower | Higher |
  | Best for | Established businesses | Start-ups, SMBs without credit-control resource |

  ---

  H2: Which Is Right for Your Business?

  Choose invoice discounting if your business has an established credit-control function, annual turnover above £500k, and you want the relationship with your customers to remain entirely with you. Discounting is also the right choice if you are in a sector where customers would view a third-party chaser negatively (professional services, for example).

  Choose invoice factoring if your business is at an earlier stage, does not have dedicated credit-control resource, or wants to outsource the overhead of chasing debtors. Factoring suits businesses where the customer-facing relationship is transactional rather than relationship-driven.

  ---

  H2: Costs Compared

  The factoring premium over discounting is roughly 0.5% to 1.5% of funded turnover. For a business with £200k monthly invoice volume, that is an extra £1,000 to £3,000 a month. For many smaller businesses without credit-control headcount, the labour saving is worth more than the extra fee.
